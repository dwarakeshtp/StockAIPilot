# Final Report - Agentic Demand Forecasting & Inventory Reorder Recommendation System

## 1. Problem

Store replenishment needs more than a demand forecast. A planner must know how much to
trust the forecast, what the inventory policy implies, and whether the recommendation makes
business sense. Spreadsheet rules ("reorder two weeks of average sales when below one week")
ignore uncertainty and lead time, and pure ML forecasts are opaque. This POC tests whether
a small team of specialised agents, with a Critic and a human in the loop, can produce
recommendations that are both **better** and **explainable**.

## 2. Solution overview

* **Data.** Walmart M5: store CA_1, FOODS category, 12 SKUs, two years of daily sales, with
  events, SNAP and prices. Inventory parameters are simulated (see
  [dataset.md](dataset.md)).
* **Agents** (orchestrated with LangGraph; see [architecture.md](architecture.md) and
  [agent_design.md](agent_design.md)):
  1. **Forecast Agent.** ARIMA, Prophet and seasonal-naive with a rolling-origin backtest,
     selection and a 7-day forecast with an 80% interval.
  2. **Risk & Uncertainty Agent.** Confidence score, anomaly and level-shift detection, risk
     level and an uncertainty multiplier.
  3. **Inventory Planning Agent.** Safety stock, reorder point, order quantity, stockout
     probability and a stock projection.
  4. **Critic Agent.** 13 rules, a verdict, one bounded revision loop and a grounded LLM
     explanation.
* **LLM.** Free OpenRouter models or Cohere, configurable via `.env` with a provider
  fallback. The LLM only writes explanations, and a grounding check rejects any number not
  present in the agent facts.
* **UI.** Streamlit with 7 pages: forecast and risk visuals, recommendations, what-if
  analysis, Critic verdicts, Approve / Modify / Reject with an audit log, agent traces and
  performance metrics.

## 3. Quantitative results

Source: [evaluation_report.md](evaluation_report.md), generated by
`scripts/run_evaluation.py`.

### 3.1 Forecast accuracy (real sales for the 7 days after the origin, 84 item-days)

| Model | MAE | RMSE | WAPE |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agent-selected | **8.56** | 14.56 | **35.8%** |
| Ensemble | 8.62 | 13.96 | 36.0% |
| Prophet | 8.75 | 14.08 | 36.6% |
| ARIMA | 9.07 | 14.87 | 37.9% |
| Seasonal-naive (baseline) | 10.17 | 18.29 | 42.5% |

The agent's per-item model selection beats every single model on MAE and WAPE, and improves
on the baseline by 16% (MAE) and 6.7 percentage points (WAPE).

### 3.2 Inventory KPI improvement (56-day replay with actual demand, 12 items)

| KPI | Agentic policy | Naive rule | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stockout days | **8** | 51 | -84% |
| Lost sales (units) | **130** | 1,591 | -92% |
| Fill rate | **99.2%** | 90.5% | +8.7 pp |
| Average inventory (units, summed over items) | **2,086** | 2,380 | -12% |
| Orders placed | 83 | 48 | more frequent, smaller orders |

The agentic policy achieves higher service with *less* stock, because safety stock scales
with forecast error, risk and lead time instead of a fixed number of days of cover. The
trade-off is more frequent ordering.

### 3.3 Agent collaboration (11 injected scenarios)

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Detection recall | 100% |
| Detection precision (excluding accepted side effects) / strict | 100% / 69% |
| Verdict accuracy | 100% |
| Grounding pass rate | 100% |
| Recall using only the Risk Agent's signals | 18% |

Without the Critic, most injected problems would reach the planner silently: data gaps,
unreliable forecasts, excessive orders driven by MOQ, stockouts before open orders arrive,
and high stockout probability. The Risk Agent alone only catches demand spikes and drops.

## 4. Qualitative evaluation

### 4.1 Explainability assessment

| Criterion | How it is met | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Every number is traceable | Numbers come from deterministic code. The LLM text is checked against facts (grounding), with a template fallback | `grounding.py`, `grounding_passed` on every result |
| Why this model? | Backtest metrics per model, skill vs naive, agreement | Forecast page, metrics table |
| Why this quantity? | SS, ROP, inventory position and formula inputs shown; projection chart | Inventory page |
| Why should I trust or doubt it? | Confidence components, anomalies, data-quality score, Critic issues with severity | Risk and Critic pages |
| What happened inside? | Per-node trace with inputs, outputs, reasoning, LLM provider and timing | Agent Trace page, `outputs/traces/*.json` |
| What if assumptions change? | What-if recomputation, re-validated by the Critic | Inventory page |

Limitations: template explanations are accurate but repetitive. LLM explanations read
better but depend on free-tier availability; the fallback keeps the system working.

### 4.2 Agent collaboration review

* **Information flow.** Forecast error drives the Risk Agent's confidence, which scales
  safety stock in the Inventory Agent. In the scenarios, changing data quality or forecast
  error visibly changed confidence, the multiplier and therefore the order quantity.
* **Feedback.** On FOODS_3_519 the Critic detected WAPE 63% and 70% ARIMA/Prophet
  disagreement and sent the Forecast Agent back to use the ensemble. Issues remained after
  one revision, so it escalated to a human instead of looping again.
* **Cross-agent consistency.** On FOODS_3_090 the Inventory Agent said HOLD (inventory
  position above ROP thanks to an open order). The Critic noticed that the open order
  arrives *after* the projected stockout and escalated, a failure mode no single agent sees.
* **Weaknesses.** The Critic is rule-based, so new failure modes need new rules. Precision
  is lower when one scenario has several real side effects (strict precision 69%).

## 5. Engineering quality

* Typed pydantic contracts between agents; LangGraph `TypedDict` state.
* SOLID: strategy pattern for rules, data sources, forecasters and LLM providers; agents
  depend on abstractions.
* Structured JSON logging (structlog), JSON traces, JSONL decision audit.
* Over 100 pytest tests (synthetic data, no network), including a headless Streamlit smoke
  test; ruff clean.
* Reproducible: seeded simulation, committed processed data, a single `settings.yaml`.

## 6. Limitations and future work

* Inventory data is simulated, and M5 records sales rather than demand. Simulation results
  are directional.
* Hold-out is a single 7-day origin over 12 SKUs, so it is not statistically conclusive.
* Free LLM tiers are rate-limited, and explanations fall back to templates when they are.
* Next steps: global LightGBM, multi-store, weather and promotions, a real ERP feed,
  learning from planner decisions, and cloud deployment (see [roadmap.md](roadmap.md)).

## 7. Conclusion

Four cooperating agents, with a Critic that validates, revises and escalates, produced
forecasts better than any single model and reorder decisions that cut simulated stockouts
by 84% while holding less inventory. Every recommendation comes with a traceable,
fact-checked explanation and a human decision step.
